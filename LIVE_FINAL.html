Try a spaza owner's evening

At closing time the owner counts six products, writes the numbers on a paper card and sends them from any phone. The system replies with what to buy tomorrow. You play the owner for one week.

A demonstration of a student design. It has not been tested with shop owners. All numbers are examples.

    For engineers: the maths behind the list (the owner never sees this)
    
d ← 0.2·x + 0.8·d    v ← 0.2·(x − d_old)² + 0.8·v

      d_eff = d·(1 + 0.25·A)

      s = d_eff·(1 + L) + Z·√((1 + L)·v + d_eff²·σL²), Z = 0.84 (bread: s ≤ 4·d_eff)

      order Q = max(1, round(d_eff·R)) when stock ≤ s; list ranked by days of cover − L, lowest first

    

    

    
x = units that left stock since the last count (last count + delivery − tonight's count), raised by 20% on a day that sold out. First evening: no learning and no checks, because there is no earlier count. Check: on the first evening, with the month-end switch off, an oil count of 3 gives s = 6.347 and an order of 17, as in the design report's worked example.

  


  

    
Good to know

    
    
      
  • Why paper and a basic phone? The card works with no phone, signal or power. The phone part uses USSD (like checking airtime), which works on any phone and needs no data bundle.
    • 
      
  • Where does the list come from? The system learns how fast each product sells in this shop from the owner's own counts, and suggests buying before a product runs out. Bread is only reordered when less than about 4 days' worth is left, because it goes stale.
    • 
      
  • What if a number is wrong? If a count is higher than the shop could have, or a much bigger drop than usual, the phone asks once to check it.
    • 
      
  • Back of the card: the owner writes each product's "order when below" number, so the card still helps on a night with no signal.
    • 
      
  • Demo limits: the starting values are typical values (medians) from a survey of 20 Soweto shops; every delivery is assumed to take 1 day; "2 Some" at "Bought?" is recorded as nothing bought; a confirmed count above the stock the shop had is treated as nothing sold; missed evenings are not shown here; the design does not say which screen shows the "order when below" numbers, so the back of the card fills itself in. The "1 Back" option on the reasons screen is added here; the design's screen does not show one.
